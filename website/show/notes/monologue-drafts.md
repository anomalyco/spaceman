# Monologue drafts (pass 2)

One continuous recording, played back from tape. Same fiction as pass 1 (Pinecrest, No. 12 is him,
No. 14's hedge, the drill from No. 10, Dana, the minutes). Each draft explains Spaceman truthfully in
its first half and answers a different question at the end: what is this recording, and why does it
exist? The picture is the same for all three until the last 15 s.

---

## Draft A: "All of it" (a custody interview)

The recording is a police interview, played back years later. The interviewer is never heard; he
repeats a question once. The reveal is a double meaning on "six feet".

> [click] Is that — is it on? The little light. Okay.
>
> I'd had some of those windows open since March. Three of them were the same note. I closed two.
>
> You asked what it does. It puts everything side by side. Nothing on top of anything else. And if you
> don't want to look at something, you hold option and shift and a number, and it goes to that number.
> Mail went to two. The internet went to three. It isn't closed. People always think it's closed. It's
> on two. There's a bar along the bottom, so you always know where everything is.
>
> I wrote a rule for Dana. Chatter opens on two. That wasn't — it's just where it goes.
>
> I returned the drill. I'd had it four years. He said I didn't need to.
>
> Four was the house. Five was the street. Nobody else wanted to take the minutes.
>
> Number fourteen's hedge was six foot two. The limit is six feet. I raised it with him twice. After the
> second time I photographed it. Every morning, six-oh-two. You have those.
>
> The twentieth. Where was I on the twentieth. I was on five. His light was on, at ten to midnight.
> There's no rule about lights. I checked. So I went over to talk to him about the hedge.
>
> I've told you what happened. I was very clear.
>
> It's six feet now.
>
> All of it.

Ending picture: as pass 1, but the last desktop is an evidence review station: his screen recording
playing as "Exhibit 12-C", beside "Interview 2 — audio".

## Draft B: "Are you recording this?" (No. 14's tape)

The listener is No. 14, at his own kitchen table, the night of the twentieth. He never answers. The
recording exists because No. 14 made it.

> [click] — your light was on. I saw it from the study. I hope it's all right. I'll be quick.
>
> I'd had some of those windows open since March. Three of them were the same note. I closed two.
>
> I got a program. It puts everything side by side, nothing on top of anything. If you don't want to
> look at something, you send it to a number. Mail went to two. The internet went to three. It isn't
> closed. It's on two. There's a bar along the bottom, so you always know where everything is.
>
> I wrote a rule for Dana's messages. They open on two. That's just where they go.
>
> I gave Mark his drill back. Four years. He said I didn't need to.
>
> Four is the house. Five is the street. You'd know that, you get the minutes.
>
> Your hedge is six foot two. The limit is six feet. We've talked about this twice. I've photographed
> it every morning since. Six-oh-two. I'm not here about the hedge.
>
> Can I sit? I'll sit.
>
> You've been taking pictures too. Of the study. I saw them. That's fine. It's good, actually. Somebody
> should keep a record.
>
> Is that — are you recording this?
>
> Good.
>
> Leave it on.

Ending picture: No. 14's desktop (pass 1's ending), now with "Recorder — Nov 20, 11:52 PM" running.

## Draft C: "Welcome to Pinecrest" (the tape in the drawer)

The tape was left for whoever buys No. 14. The listener is the new owner, and, by the cut to
"For your windows.", the visitor.

> [click] Okay. Hello.
>
> I'll explain how I do things. People like to know, when they're new.
>
> I'd had some of those windows open since March. Three of them were the same note. I closed two.
>
> Then everything went side by side. Nothing on top of anything. If you don't want to look at something,
> you send it to another number. Mail went to two. The internet went to three. It isn't closed. It's on
> two. There's a bar along the bottom, so you always know where everything is.
>
> I wrote a rule for my friend's messages. They open on two.
>
> I returned the drill to number ten. He said I didn't need to.
>
> Four is the house. Five is the street. Nobody else wanted to take the minutes, so I take the minutes.
>
> The man who had your house had a hedge. It was six foot two. The limit is six feet. I raised it with
> him twice. After that I photographed it every morning at six-oh-two. A hundred and sixty mornings.
>
> He left a light on, most nights.
>
> He doesn't live there now.
>
> The hedge is six feet. I'd keep it that way.
>
> [pause] I'm across the street. Number twelve.
>
> Welcome to number fourteen.
>
> Your light's on.

Ending picture: the pull-back leaves his house and crosses the street; the last desktop is the new
owner's at No. 14, a cassette player window open ("found in kitchen drawer"), and its doorbell feed
shows No. 12's study lit.
