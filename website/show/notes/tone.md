# Tone (current)

Kit, Nov 20, replacing "cartoon villainy only":

> It doesn't have to be cartoony villainy... it's more funny if it's serious, like a good indie A24
> movie. It'll be funny because of how serious it is.

And the touchstone: Dr. Strangelove. Everyone stays procedural and sincere; the laughs come from calm,
official language applied to something monstrous. Nobody in the piece knows it's a comedy.

Rules I'm holding every line and window title to:

- **The straight-face test.** Would a completely serious man say or write this? If a line knows it
  is a joke, cut it.
- **No winking, no moustache.** No moon, no henchmen, no lasers, no laugh, no shouting. Unhinged
  means colder and more certain, not louder.
- **Specific and mundane.** Hedges, bins, minutes, rotas, the drill he borrowed. The darkness is
  control and certainty in one fictional man's life. Nothing about real people, groups or events,
  and nothing that reads as instructions for harm.
- **Picture and sound escalate, the voice doesn't.** Pace of switching, what the windows hold, the
  light in the room, silence.
- **Product truth first.** Tiling, workspaces, a rule, the bar, shown clearly in the first half.
  Later fiction may lean on real semantics (rules match apps and window titles; "ignore" means
  Spaceman leaves a window alone) but never claims features Spaceman lacks.
- **The ending lands like a last shot**, then a hard cut to the download.

old brief and is kept as history. Useful survivors: the desktop pile as the pre-start still, the
visitor's ⌥1 doing the first tile, "It's not gone. It's on two.", no early tell for the ending.
