# Three drafts, straight tone (v2)

Shared frame for all three: one man's Mac, seen straight on, late at night. The screen is the only
light in the room; a faint warm vignette at the edges is the room. The cursor is his hand. The
narrator is Kit's voice in first person, close to the mic, unhurried, never raised. Invented apps:
**Jot** (notes), **Postbox** (mail), **Chatter** (chat), **Daybook** (calendar), **Browsy**
(browser), **Doorbell** (camera), **Sheets**, **Call** (video calls).

Pre-start still, shared: the pile. Window titles are sincere: `notes`, `notes (1)`, `notes — real`,
`Untitled 47`, `Postbox — 4,812 unread`, `Browsy — 212 tabs`, `Screenshot 2026-03-02 at 11.48.07 PM`,
`how to feel on top of things`, `Recipes`, `return drill??`. A yellow sticky: `organize notes`. Menu
bar: Tue 11:48 PM. A keycap: **⌥1**. The visitor's ⌥1 (or tap) starts the show and is the first
tile. Nothing plays before it.

The first 40 seconds are the same product demo in all three, voiced differently:

- ⌥1: every window tiles on workspace 1 at once. A dense grid. Two seconds of silence.
- He closes duplicates one at a time; the tiles reflow to fill the space each time.
- ⌥⇧2 sends Postbox to workspace 2, ⌥⇧3 sends Browsy to 3. The bar at the bottom shows 1, 2 and 3
  as small layouts with app icons, the current one larger, the marker stretching as it slides.
- A rule: `Chatter → workspace 2`. A new Chatter window opens on 1 and slides off to 2.
- The visitor can press ⌥1, ⌥2, ⌥3 during this stretch and the show follows.

## Draft D: "A place for everything"

**Engine.** A diary. He describes, in the tone of someone recovering from something, the small
improvements in his life. The improvements are sincere and the words never change register. What
changes is what's in the windows: a house, then a street, then people. He never notices anything
is wrong.

**Ending.** He's used nine workspaces and kept one free. His cursor leaves his screen, crosses the
bezel into the web page, and quietly tiles the page the visitor is reading.

**0:00 Relief.** ⌥1. Grid. Silence.
- "I'd had some of those windows open since March."
- (closing duplicates) "Notes. Notes one. Notes, real." beat. "I kept the real one."
- (⌥⇧2, ⌥⇧3) "Mail lives on two now. The internet lives on three." beat. "I visit."
- (rule) "I wrote a rule. Chatter opens on two." (the window slides away) "It's not gone. It's on
  two."

**0:40 Order.** The clock and date start moving between switches: Wed 6:02 AM, Thu 6:00 AM. Jot:
`✓ dentist ✓ taxes ✓ return drill`.
- "I returned the drill. I'd had it four years. He said I didn't need to."
- "I get up at six now."
- ⌥4, a new workspace. Tiles: `Groceries — Standing Order`, `Laundry — Rota`, `Dinner (Fri) —
  Agenda`. "I gave the house a workspace. It runs better with a little structure."

**0:58 The street.** ⌥5. `Residents' Committee — Minutes`, `Bins — Collection Compliance`, a Doorbell
feed of the street at night. Switching gets quicker; the wallpaper loses its colour.
- "Nobody wanted to take the minutes. So I take the minutes."
- Titles multiply: `No. 14 — Hedge`, `No. 14 — Hedge (2)`, `No. 14 — Hedge (Daily)`.
- "Number fourteen's hedge is one metre ninety. The limit is one eighty. I've photographed it every
  morning since June."
- `Committee — Restructuring Proposal` → `Committee — Membership (1)`. "The committee is more
  efficient now. There's less disagreement."

**1:18 People.** ⌥6: four Call tiles of equal size, the people in them drawn as still silhouettes.
- "My family is on six. Everyone gets exactly the same amount of space."
- The rules list scrolls: `Postbox → 2`, `Chatter → 2`, `Doorbell → 5`, `Call → 6`,
  `Chatter, title "Dana" → ignore`. No comment from him.
- ⌥7, ⌥8, ⌥9 too fast to read. Then stillness. "There are ten workspaces. I've used nine."
- beat. "I'd kept one free."

**1:35 Last shot.** The cursor drifts to the edge of his screen and keeps going, past the bezel,
onto the page. It picks up the page's heading and drops it into workspace 10. It fits exactly.
- "You have a lot of windows open."
- beat. "I can help with that."

Hard cut. Calm download card. **Ten workspaces. Please use them for windows.**

## Draft E: "Procedure"

**Engine.** The rules are policy, and he reads them into the record. His narration sounds like a
man giving testimony at a calm inquiry: numbered, precise, polite. Each rule is real Spaceman
syntax and is honest about what it does. The comedy is the distance between the dry language and
what the rules are being used for. The first-match order becomes doctrine.

**Ending.** His family sends a Daybook invite: `Talk — everyone (no agenda)`. He reads it, writes one
more rule, and the invite slides off to workspace 10. He switches to workspace 1, which is calm and
clean.

**0:00 Relief.** ⌥1. Grid. Silence.
- "On the second of March I pressed Option one."
- (closing duplicates) "Three of the windows were the same note. I closed two."
- (⌥⇧2, ⌥⇧3) "Mail was assigned to workspace two. The internet to three."
- (rule) "Rule one. Chatter opens on workspace two." (slides away) "It isn't closed. It's on two.
  That distinction became important."

**0:40 Rules.** A Rules window stays on screen, rows adding themselves with each line.
- "Rule two. Daybook, workspace four." "Rule three. Doorbell, workspace five."
- "Rules are checked in order. The first one that matches is the one that applies." beat. "I found
  that very calming."
- Workspaces fill: groceries on a rota, the residents' committee minutes, a Doorbell feed.

**1:00 Titles.** He discovers that rules can match a window title.
- "A rule can also match a window's title."
- `Chatter, title "Mum" → float`. "Rule nine. My mother floats. She's above the layout. I think she'd
  like that."
- `Postbox, title "Committee" → workspace 5`. `Chatter, title "Dana" → ignore`. "Rule eleven. Dana is
  ignored." beat. "In Spaceman, that means it leaves her windows exactly where they are. She can
  stay wherever she likes."
- The list scrolls faster than it can be read. The light drops. "Rule thirty-one." "Rule
  thirty-two." "Rule thirty-three."

**1:25 Talk.** A Daybook invite appears on workspace 1: `Talk — everyone (no agenda)`. Every invitee
has accepted. He looks at it for four seconds of silence.
- He types: `Daybook, title "Talk" → workspace 10`. "Rule thirty-four."
- The invite slides off to 10. In the bar, workspace 10 is a single small tile. ⌥1: his desk, clean.
- "Everything is where it should be."

Hard cut. Calm download card. **Rules match apps and window titles.**

## Draft F: "Ten"

**Engine.** Ten chapters, one per workspace. The bar is the film's timeline: each new workspace is
a chapter card (`2.`, `3.`) and a jump in time, shown by the menu bar date. Almost no narration:
one or two lines a chapter, and long silent holds where we read his screen. The escalation is pure
picture: what each workspace holds, and how the earlier ones quietly change when he passes back
through them.

**Ending.** Workspace 10 is the end of the film. The camera pulls back from his screen and we see
it is one tile in someone else's layout: a calmer, tidier desktop, workspace 7, titled
`Him — Observations`. The marker in that stranger's bar is already moving.

**0:00 1. Relief.** ⌥1. Grid. Silence.
- "It took about a second."
- Duplicates close, Postbox and Browsy move to 2 and 3, the Chatter rule. "It's not gone. It's on
  two." (the visitor's free switching stretch is here)

**0:40 4. House.** `Groceries — Standing Order`, `Laundry — Rota`. "The house has a workspace."

**0:50 5. Street.** `Residents' Committee — Minutes`, Doorbell. Ten seconds of silence while
`No. 14 — Hedge (Daily)` fills with dated photos, one per morning.

**1:02 6. Family.** Four equal Call tiles. "Everyone gets the same amount of space."

**1:10 7, 8, 9.** Each chapter shorter than the last; we glimpse `Committee — Membership (1)`,
`Chatter, title "Dana" → ignore`, a Sheets window titled `Contingency (Household)`. Passing back
through 4, the Groceries window now reads `Groceries — Standing Order (Revised, Final)`.

**1:25 10.** Empty. He sits with it. "I wasn't sure what to put here." Then the long pull-back.
Silence, then one soft sound: a key press that isn't his.

Hard cut. Calm download card. **Spaceman. Works on any number of displays.**
