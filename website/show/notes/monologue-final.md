From the start? All right.

I want to explain how I keep things. Then it'll make sense.

I'd had some of those windows open since March. February, some of them. Three of them were, uh... the same note. I closed two.

Then I installed a small program. It's called Spaceman.

And the windows tiled themselves. Side by side. Nothing on top of anything. Nothing behind anything. You could see all of it at once... I tasted the sweet flesh of efficiency.

Mail went to workspace two. The browser went to three. I went back to make sure, and they were just... there. Exactly where I'd put them. Nothing was ever lost again. There's a bar along the bottom, so you always know where everything is. You're never more than one key away from anything.

Then I made a rule. Chatter opens on two. You don't have to decide anymore. It just goes. I did read both messages.

I gave Mark his drill back. Four years. It's a better drill than mine, actually. But it had a place, and the place was his.

Four is the house. Five is the street. Nobody else wanted to take the minutes.

Number fourteen's hedge was six foot two. The limit is six feet. We'd talked about it... twice. I photographed it every morning after that. A hundred and sixty mornings, and I could find any one of them in a second. Any morning you like.

I sent the minutes round again. He was the only one who replied.

There was a photograph attached. It took me a minute to recognise the study. From his side.

Three of them signed the same note. About the cameras. I closed two.

Sixteen was in March. Mark was in June, the day I gave him his drill back. It was quick, and it was very clean. Nobody missed either of them for a week. Six is downstairs. They're both on six.

Everything else had a place. He was the last thing.

I went over just before midnight. His light was on. He took a while to come down. I'd already put the plastic down. Side by side. Nothing on top of anything. Nothing behind anything.

It didn't take long. It never does.

He's on six now. With the others.
